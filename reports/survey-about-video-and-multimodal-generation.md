# Comprehensive Survey of Video and Multimodal Generation

## TL;DR
- Video generation architectures are shifting toward Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and unified multimodal token spaces that jointly model text, image, audio, and video [1][2][3].
- Models like Sora and Open-Sora leverage patch-based latent representations and spatial-temporal scaling to simulate complex real-world dynamics [1][4].
- Autoregressive systems such as Chameleon, VILA-U, and VideoMAR integrate visual understanding and generation into a single causal framework using continuous or discrete tokens [2][5][6].
- Efficiency breakthroughs including token routing, subspace clustering (SCOPE), and wavelet-flow VAEs enable high-definition generation while managing massive computational overhead [7][8][9].
- Persistent challenges involve physical law violations, prompt-injection vulnerabilities, and inconsistent alignment with human preference benchmarks [10].

## Background
The field of video and multimodal generation has undergone a profound transformation, moving from early constrained pixel-space models and decoupled text-to-image pipelines to unified, high-definition world simulators [1][11]. Historically, generating coherent video sequences was bottlenecked by the scarcity of paired text-video training data and the extreme computational cost of modeling dense spatial-temporal tensors [11][12]. Modern approaches bypass these limitations by leveraging pre-trained text-to-image models for static appearance, coupled with specialized temporal convolution, attention layers, or factorized latent representations learned from unsupervised video footage [11][13]. Furthermore, the transition toward native early-fusion transformer architectures allows models to process text, image, audio, and video within shared vocabulary spaces, bridging the traditional divide between perceptual understanding and generative synthesis [2][5][3].

## Diffusion Transformers and Spatiotemporal Architectures
Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have largely replaced traditional U-Net backbones in state-of-the-art video generation [1][4]. Sora trains text-conditional diffusion models jointly on videos and images of variable durations, resolutions, and aspect ratios by extracting visual patches compressed into a lower-dimensional latent space [1]. To overcome data scarcity, Make-A-Video learns visual appearances from paired text-image data and temporal motion from unsupervised video footage by stacking 1D temporal attention and temporal convolution after pre-trained spatial layers [11]. Similarly, VideoGen employs an off-the-shelf text-to-image model to generate a reference image, followed by a cascaded latent diffusion module and flow-based temporal upsampling [13]. Open-Sora further democratizes efficient video generation by utilizing a Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer and a 3D autoencoder, exploring attention head specialization across model scales [4].

## Unified Multimodal Autoregressive Models
Alongside diffusion paradigms, autoregressive frameworks and unified foundation models have advanced joint understanding and generation [2][5][6]. Chameleon uses a mixed-modal early-fusion token-based model with a unified tokenization framework, converting text and visual modalities into a shared vocabulary of discrete tokens to handle tasks from image captioning to long-form mixed-modal generation [2]. VILA-U integrates video, image, and language understanding and generation within a single causal language modeling objective using a unified token space [5]. Gemini models are trained jointly across image, audio, video, and text data using a large context window where video frames can be interleaved natively with text and audio [3][14]. Additionally, VideoMAR introduces an efficient decoder-only autoregressive model for video generation using continuous tokens, temporal causality, spatial bi-directionality, and 3D rotary embeddings [6].

## Tokenization, Conditioning, and Efficiency Enhancements
Managing the quadratic computational cost of dense spatiotemporal token sequences has driven innovations in tokenization, conditioning, and sparse attention [15][16][17][7][8][9]. Divot leverages a diffusion process for self-supervised video representation learning, decoding videos from continuous tokenizer representations modeled via a Gaussian Mixture Model [15]. For super-resolution and compression, TRaM-VSR proposes a Token Routing and Merging framework that isolates dynamic objects and structural boundaries by fusing motion-sensitive temporal cues with semantic text similarity [16]. Group-of-Latents (GoL) partitions latent streams into intra $I$-latents and inter $P$-latents, compressing perceptual anchors while synthesizing temporal dynamics at zero additional bitrate cost [7]. SCOPE combines 3D-RoPE-aligned key subspace clustering with online per-head Top-$k$ estimation to achieve up to a $1.99\times$ end-to-end speedup on 720p HunyuanVideo [8]. To optimize data pipelines, Open-Sora Plan employs WF-VAE with multi-level wavelet transforms and a Min-Max Token strategy across resolutions and durations [9]. For video editing, RVEDiT introduces Granularity-Routed Token Conditioning and Reference-Anchored Attention Alignment to route learnable editing tokens to shallow blocks [17].

## Evaluation Benchmarks and Open Challenges
Evaluating generative video models has evolved significantly beyond traditional metrics like FID and FVD, which often correlate poorly with human judgment [18]. VBench evaluates video models across 16 dimensions including motion smoothness, temporal consistency, and subject identity consistency [10][18]. Newer benchmarks like AIGVE-60K introduce the LOVE metric using Large Multimodal Models to disentangle perceptual quality and text-video correspondence [10]. Despite these advances, critical challenges remain: state-of-the-art models frequently violate basic physical laws such as momentum conservation and rigid-body collisions [10]. Furthermore, models exhibit severe security vulnerabilities, such as T2VAttack where minor word modifications degrade semantic fidelity, alongside cross-modal alignment failures and provenance tracking hurdles addressed by frameworks like VidStamp [10].

## Trends and Open Problems
The past two years have witnessed a decisive shift toward scale, architectural unification, and efficiency in video generation [1][4][2][6]. Key unsolved problems include the mitigation of physical law hallucinations, robust safety and watermarking against adversarial prompt injections, and overcoming the heavy compute limits of long-form video generation [10]. While data curation pipelines have improved through aesthetic scoring and jump-cut detection, the field continues to debate the optimal balance between synthetic data scaling, wavelet compression efficiency, and open-source democratization [4][10][9].
