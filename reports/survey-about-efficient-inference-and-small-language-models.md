# Survey on Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
- Small Language Models (SLMs) achieve extreme compression via mixed-precision quantization-aware distillation and adaptive token quantization, slashing storage requirements and boosting decoding speed [1][2].
- Advanced training techniques like Game of Regularizer (GoR) and reverse KL divergence distillation solve loss-balancing conflicts and eliminate probability overestimation in distilled student models [3][4].
- Hybrid architectures combining Transformers, State-Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba, and Mixture of Experts (MoE) modules overcome quadratic memory scaling and KV cache bottlenecks [5][6][7].
- Specialized architectural designs such as Block Transformers and routing optimizations enable long-context modeling and multi-fold increases in inference throughput [8].
- Hardware-software co-design, disaggregated FPGA/CXL caches, eDRAM edge accelerators, and hierarchical quantized speculative decoding deliver massive speedups and energy savings [9][10][11][12][13].

## Background
The rapid proliferation of large language models has driven unprecedented demand for efficient inference methodologies, particularly as deployment shifts toward edge devices, smartphones, and resource-constrained servers. While massive models deliver formidable generalization capabilities, their massive parameter counts and quadratic memory scaling impose prohibitive computational overheads, high latency, and excessive energy consumption. Small Language Models (SLMs) have consequently emerged as a vital research paradigm, offering high performance while remaining suitable for deployment on hardware with strict memory and power footprints [7]. 

However, deploying SLMs efficiently requires tackling fundamental hardware-software bottlenecks, including memory-bound autoregressive decoding, large key-value (KV) cache memory footprints, and sub-optimal quantization trade-offs [1][12]. Recent breakthroughs spanning advanced quantization-aware training, hybrid state-space model architectures, hardware-accelerated KV caching, and speculative decoding have transformed the landscape of efficient inference. This survey synthesizes these methodologies, highlighting how architectural and algorithmic innovations harmonize to unlock scalable, low-latency, and energy-efficient language model serving.

## Quantization and Knowledge Distillation for Small Language Models
Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) methods frequently experience severe quality degradation when quantizing weights, activations, and KV caches simultaneously to ultra-low bit widths below 8 bits [1]. This performance drop stems primarily from information distortion within quantized attention maps, manifested as disparate statistical distributions between quantized queries and keys [1]. To overcome these limitations, advanced quantization-aware training (QAT) frameworks like EdgeQAT employ token importance-aware adaptive methods to dynamically assign distinct bit widths across tokens, yielding up to a 2.37x on-device speedup over FP16 baselines [1].

Further advancing compression limits, frameworks such as EdgeRazor integrate mixed-precision quantization-aware distillation, adaptive feature distillation, and entropy-aware KL divergence to achieve extreme footprint reductions [2]. Specifically, EdgeRazor's 1.88-bit configuration outperforms conventional 3-bit precisions and surpasses leading 2-bit PTQ methods by 11.3 points while requiring a fraction of the training budget [2]. For instance, a 1.58-bit Qwen3-0.6B model compressed via EdgeRazor shrinks storage from 1.41 GB to 0.28 GB and accelerates decoding by 15.1 times compared to the 16-bit baseline [2].

Balancing task-specific supervision and distillation objectives during low-bit QAT remains notoriously challenging due to heterogeneous gradient magnitudes [3]. The Game of Regularizer (GoR) addresses this by introducing a learnable regularization method that dynamically balances task-specific and knowledge distillation objectives using only two trainable parameters, mitigating gradient conflict and improving convergence [3]. Furthermore, combining GoR with multi-teacher ensembles in QAT-EKD-GoR enables small quantized models to approach or even surpass full-precision model performance [3]. To prevent student models from overestimating low-probability regions of teacher distributions, MiniLLM employs reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence (KLD) during knowledge distillation, resulting in generated responses with reduced exposure bias, superior calibration, and enhanced long-text generation capabilities across model scales ranging from 120M to 13B parameters [4].

## Architectural Innovations: Hybrid SSMs, MoE, and Optimized Transformers
Standard transformer architectures suffer from extreme memory and compute demands during long-context generation due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention and the linear growth of the key-value (KV) cache [5][7]. To address this, hybrid architectures such as Jamba integrate Transformer layers, Mamba state-space model (SSM) layers, and mixture-of-experts (MoE) modules into a unified design [5]. Jamba-1.5-Mini utilizes 12B active parameters out of 52B total parameters, successfully fitting within a single 80GB GPU while interleaving attention and Mamba layers to preserve in-context learning and induction head capabilities [5].

Mixture of Experts (MoE) models offer expansive capacity paired with conditional computational efficiency, yet present severe deployment challenges regarding memory bandwidth and expert routing overhead [6]. System-level and model-level optimizations—including expert parallelism, refined routing strategies, and expert-offloading mechanisms—allow sparse activation patterns to fetch required experts selectively from CPU memory or SSD storage rather than loading entire parameter sets layer by layer [6].

At the lightweight end of the spectrum, specialized decoder-only architectures like TinyLLaMA leverage FlashAttention to optimize memory overhead at 1.1B parameters, while MobileLLM implements block-wise weight sharing and grouped-query attention to maximize mobile deployment scalability [7]. Alternative self-attention mechanisms further alleviate scaling limits: Reformer reduces quadratic complexity to logarithmic complexity ($O(N \log N)$), Linear Attention enables linear dot-product processing, and Mamba introduces input-dependent state-space transitions for linear complexity scaling [7]. Additionally, the Block Transformer employs a hierarchical global-to-local modeling paradigm, where lower layers aggregate tokens into fixed-size blocks for coarse-grained global context and upper layers decode tokens locally using lightweight KV caches, achieving 10-20x higher inference throughput than vanilla transformers while maintaining equivalent perplexity and zero-shot performance [8].

## Hardware-Software Co-Design and KV Cache Optimization
Autoregressive decoding is fundamentally bound by memory bandwidth, where transferring model parameters and KV caches from High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) to processor caches per token step severely underutilizes computational units [10][12]. Hardware-software co-design strategies address this by integrating dedicated memory technologies and disaggregated acceleration frameworks. For example, CXL-SpecKV utilizes Compute Express Link (CXL) interconnects and FPGA accelerators to offload and disaggregate KV caches into remote FPGA memory, expanding capacity by 4x to 8x while incorporating an FPGA-accelerated compression engine operating at 800 MHz with a 1.6 TB/s bandwidth [9]. Furthermore, CXL-SpecKV employs a lightweight 128KB LSTM model running on the FPGA with sub-10 microsecond latency to predict and prefetch future token cache entries with 95% top-4 accuracy [9].

For edge computing environments, the Kelle accelerator utilizes embedded DRAM (eDRAM) as primary storage, pairing it with an attention-based eviction and recomputation policy (AERP) and a two-dimensional adaptive refresh policy (2DRP) via a dedicated eDRAM controller and systolic evictor [10]. This hardware co-design yields a 3.9x speedup and 4.5x energy savings over baseline solutions with negligible impact on accuracy [10]. Similarly, hierarchical 4-bit quantized KV cache designs optimize memory footprints on edge hardware during autoregressive generation [13].

## Speculative Decoding and Inference Acceleration
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation via a draft-then-verify paradigm, where multiple future tokens are rapidly generated by a lightweight drafter and verified in parallel by the target large language model [11][12]. Empirical evaluations across small language model families (1-2B parameters) reveal that draft generation overhead, rather than draft quality alone, constitutes the primary bottleneck limiting acceleration in small models [11]. While traditional independent drafting often fails due to architectural mismatches in available drafters, self-drafting strategies—such as incorporating lightweight feed-forward neural (FFN) decoding heads (e.g., Medusa) atop the decoder—and retrieval-based methods with minimal computational overhead yield consistent inference speedups [11][12]. Advanced self-speculative frameworks like QuantSpec further integrate hierarchical 4-bit quantized KV caches and quantized weights to maximize memory efficiency and execution speed during edge deployment [13].

## Trends and Open Problems
Recent advances in efficient inference for small language models have clearly shifted from isolated software heuristics to holistic hardware-software co-design, hybrid SSM-Transformer architectures, and adaptive low-bit quantization. Over the past two years, the integration of state-space models like Mamba with sparse Mixture-of-Experts has effectively broken the quadratic attention barrier, while disaggregated CXL memory and FPGA-based prefetching have largely mitigated memory bandwidth bottlenecks in datacenter and edge deployments alike.

Despite these milestones, several critical challenges remain unsolved and heavily disputed within the community:
1. **Draft Overhead vs. Accuracy Trade-offs in Speculative Decoding**: While speculative decoding offers theoretical speedups, for very small language models (1-2B parameters), the compute overhead of generating draft tokens frequently outweighs the verification gains [11]. Finding optimal, zero-overhead drafting mechanisms remains an open question.
2. **Loss-Balancing Conflicts in Low-Bit QAT**: Training models at extreme sub-2-bit precisions (such as 1.58-bit) introduces severe gradient conflicts between task objectives and distillation losses [3]. Current learnable regularizers and token-adaptive methods alleviate this, but universal stability across diverse model topologies is yet unproven.
3. **Generalization Limitations of Hybrid Architectures**: While hybrid models like Jamba successfully combine Mamba and Transformer layers, determining the optimal ratio and interleaving pattern of state-space versus attention layers across varying task domains remains empirical rather than principled.
4. **Hardware Portability and Standardization**: Advanced co-design paradigms relying on custom eDRAM controllers [10], CXL disaggregated FPGA memory [9], and hardware-specific GGUF quantization formats [2] face fragmentation challenges when deploying portable SLMs across heterogeneous consumer edge devices and mobile hardware ecosystems.
