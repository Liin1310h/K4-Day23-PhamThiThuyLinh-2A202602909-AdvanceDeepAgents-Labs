# A Comprehensive Survey of World Models in Artificial Intelligence

## TL;DR
- World models serve as internal simulators that learn environmental structures and dynamics, enabling agents to predict, plan, and reason within learned latent representations [1].
- Systems like DreamerV3 and NE-Dreamer leverage recurrent state-space models and temporal transformers for model-based reinforcement learning, mastering complex tasks without human curricula [2][3].
- Video-based generative models such as Sora, Genie, and GameNGen scale diffusion transformers and autoregressive architectures to simulate high-fidelity interactive environments and real-time game engines [4][5][6].
- Major challenges remain, including compounding errors in long-horizon rollouts, memory bottlenecks in autoregressive video generation, and fragmented task-specific integrations [7][8][9][10][11].

## Background
World models represent a foundational paradigm shift in artificial intelligence, acting as internal simulators that capture the underlying structures, transition dynamics, and rules of complex environments [1]. Rather than relying solely on reactive policies, agents equipped with world models can anticipate future states, evaluate counterfactual actions, and plan long-horizon strategies entirely within learned latent representations [1]. This paradigm is central to advancing artificial general intelligence across reinforcement learning, robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive video generation [1]. While early approaches focused on narrow environments, recent breakthroughs in deep learning have enabled scalable, foundation-scale world models capable of processing unlabelled video data, scaling diffusion transformers, and rendering immersive interactive environments at real-time frame rates [4][5][6].

## Foundational Architectures and Methodologies
Modern world models span multiple architectural paradigms, encompassing recurrent state-space models (RSSMs), latent dynamics predictors, and transformer-based video tokenizers [1][2][3]. DreamerV3 exemplifies model-based reinforcement learning by learning a compact environmental model from replayed experience using an observation encoder, an RSSM, an observation decoder, a reward predictor, and a continuation predictor [2]. To ensure stability, DreamerV3 employs observation symlog transformations, Kullback-Leibler balance with free bits, categorical unimix, and a symexp two-hot loss, allowing it to solve diverse control tasks and Minecraft from scratch without human data [2]. Similarly, NE-Dreamer advances model-based reinforcement learning by utilizing a temporal transformer to predict next-step encoder embeddings directly, eliminating the need for observation decoders or auxiliary supervision [3]. Broadly, world models are organized along multi-axis taxonomies covering architectural choices, training methods, reasoning mechanisms, and application settings [1].

## Video Generation and Interactive Simulators
Scaling video generation models has catalyzed the development of powerful general-purpose world simulators and interactive virtual environments [4][5][6]. Sora functions as a text-conditional diffusion transformer trained jointly on variable-duration videos and images by compressing raw video into a lower-dimensional latent space and decomposing representations into spacetime transformer patches [5]. When scaled, Sora exhibits emergent world simulation properties such as 3D consistency, long-range coherence, object permanence, and interactive state rendering [5]. Complementing this, Genie operates as an 11 billion parameter foundation world model trained in an unsupervised manner from unlabelled Internet gameplay videos without ground-truth action labels [4]. Genie utilizes a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model, and a scalable latent action model to enable frame-by-frame user-prompted interactive virtual worlds [4]. Furthermore, GameNGen demonstrates that diffusion models can serve as real-time neural game engines for complex titles like DOOM, maintaining high visual quality and interactive frame rates over long trajectories [6].

## Applications in Robotics and Control
World models have become increasingly vital for robotic policy learning and closed-loop evaluation [2][10]. By simulating environmental feedback internally, robotic agents can explore action strategies safely in latent space before executing them in the physical world [1][2]. However, utilizing video world models for generalist robot policy evaluation presents distinct hurdles, notably compounding world-model errors that distort rollouts during multi-step execution [10]. To overcome such limitations and achieve reliable closed-loop evaluation, frameworks like RoboWorld introduce specialized techniques such as Step Forcing, which combines anchored and one-step self-forwarded contexts to align action-observation dynamics and reduce train-test mismatch [10].

## Challenges, Limitations, and Future Research Directions
Despite rapid progress, world models face critical theoretical and practical bottlenecks that hinder their generalization and deployment [7][8][9][10][11]. Current research often remains fragmented, characterized by task-specific integration rather than unified theoretical frameworks that robustly synthesize perception, interaction, and reasoning [9]. Surveys by Zhu et al. and Ding et al. emphasize that existing models struggle to simultaneously master current world understanding and long-horizon future prediction, suffering from severe compute inefficiency and susceptibility to error accumulation [7][8]. Moreover, autoregressive video generation encounters a fundamental temporal persistence memory problem: as sequences expand within bounded context windows, vital historical context regarding entity identities, spatial layouts, and causal interventions is lost [11]. Future research directions emphasize developing normative unified frameworks, robust physical grounding, and advanced memory architectures to ensure long-horizon consistency in world simulation [7][8][9][11].
